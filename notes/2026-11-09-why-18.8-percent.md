# Why 18.8% — the Feynman explanation

*Written 2026-11-09 for the operator, who asked why the number is what it is. Every figure here re-derives
from `benchmarks/results/2026-11-08-aw116/` (see `scripts/refresh_aw116.py`).*

## One sentence

**The model is a one-click agent.** It can open an app, tap one thing, and declare done — and it does that
correctly often enough to win 15 tasks. The moment a task needs a second capability in sequence (type a name,
then save; pick a date, then confirm), it stops working, and that is 65 of the 80 episodes we could judge.

## The number, and why it is not a surprise

| | |
|---|---|
| AC-v2 per-step accuracy | **64.07%** |
| `P(task) = p^n` at 8 steps | **2.8%** |
| measured, scorable episodes | **18.8% (15/80)** |

18.8% is *higher* than the naive prediction, and the reason is diagnostic rather than lucky: most AndroidWorld
tasks are shorter than 8 steps, and the model's per-step accuracy on **easy** screens is far above its average
on the hard AC-v2 mix. The product rule does not say "you will score 2.8%"; it says "you cannot score well if
your per-step rate is 64% and the task is long". We did not.

## The one fact that explains almost all of it

Split the 80 scorable episodes by a single observable — **did the model ever emit `type_text`?**

| | n | successes | median steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| emitted `type_text` at least once | **14** | **1** | **25** (the step budget) |
| never emitted it | **66** | **14** | **12** |

**Read that again.** Every episode where the model had to type something burned the full 25-step budget and
failed, with one exception. Every episode where it never had to type finished in half the steps and succeeded
14 times out of 66.

That is not a grounding problem and not a calibration problem. **Typing is where the model has no competence at
all**, and 18% of the suite needs it.

## What "no competence at typing" actually looks like

Take `ContactsAddContact`: create a contact for "Emily Mohammed" with a phone number.

```
i=0  open_app contacts      conf 0.996   ✓
i=1  click                  conf 0.833
i=2  click                  conf 0.993
i=3  click                  conf 0.989
i=4  type_text              conf 0.829   <- it DOES type, once
i=5  scroll                 conf 0.583
i=6..21  click x16          conf 0.77-0.93
```

It types once and then never returns to the form. It does not "fail to see" the field — it typed into it. What
it cannot do is **come back**. The next 16 clicks are a search that has no model of what it already completed.

Across all 104 finished episodes: **24 `type_text` actions out of 1,394 emitted (1.7%)** —
1,443 step rows exist, of which 49 are `status` declarations that execute nothing. On a suite where most
contacts/notes/expense tasks require text entry.

## Why the two failure buckets look like two problems but are one

| bucket | n | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| non-terminating timeout | **34** | clicked until the budget ran out |
| premature `status` | **31** | declared done at median step 7 |

These describe **how an episode ended**, not **why it failed**. A misclick that then burns 25 steps is filed as
"timeout". So the split is a description of the model's *stopping behaviour*, not an error taxonomy.

What it does tell us is that the model has **no progress signal**: sometimes it thinks it is finished at step 4
when it is not, sometimes it never thinks it is finished at all. Same missing readout, opposite symptom. This is
why a threshold on `p(action)` cannot fix it (measured 2026-11-07: a val-fitted `p(status)` gate was
monotone-harmful at every τ).

## The one thing that is genuinely good

**Zero invalid actions in 1,394 emitted.** Every action the model chose was representable and executable.
Compare the 8-task run earlier, which reported **28.2% invalid** — and that turned out to be entirely the
empty-`app_name` read slot, not the model. Fix the read slot and the action *format* is perfect.

And: `0.0%` is a real zero — every step row carries the `invalid_action` field explicitly
(1,493 rows across all files including superseded retries; 1,443 after task-level dedupe).

So: **the interface is solved, the composition is not.** That distinction is the paper.

## Where 18.8% sits, honestly

| model | params | AndroidWorld |
|---|---|---|
| Human | — | 80.0% |
| MAI-UI-2B **SFT only** | 2B | **45.1%** |
| Qwen3-VL-2B | 2B | 36.4% |
| M3A (GPT-4 Turbo + a11y) | — | 30.6% |
| Ferret-UI-Lite-3B | 3B | 28.0% |
| **this model** | 1.92B | **18.8%** |

Below every published baseline, including the 2B ones. That is the finding, and it should be stated plainly
rather than buried.

## Why the gap is 28 points and not fixable here

MAI-UI's own Table 9 separates SFT from RL:

| | end-to-end | per-step |
|---|---|---|
| MAI-UI-2B **SFT** | 45.1% | ~~92.3%~~ derived, not reported |
| MAI-UI-2B **RL** | 49.1% | ~~93.1%~~ derived, not reported |

**Online RL is worth +0.8 per-step points at 2B.** Their SFT checkpoint already runs at 92.3%. So the 28-point
gap to our 64.07% is the **supervision**, not the RL:

- multi-step trajectory corpora (GUI-Odyssey: 8,334 cross-app episodes) vs our 15k single-step decisions
- a live-emulator task-generation + MLLM-judge rejection-sampling loop vs none
- a 50-step training horizon vs no horizon at all
- a 12-action space including `ask_user` vs our 8

We trained on **single-step** decisions and evaluated on **multi-step** tasks. That mismatch is structural and
cannot be closed by more data of the same shape, more seeds, or better hyperparameters.

## What would actually move it

Ranked by expected value per GPU-hour, given one 4090:

1. **Multi-step trajectory data (GUI-Odyssey).** This is the only lever that addresses the actual defect — the
   model has never seen a task as a sequence. Nothing else on this list matters until this is done.
2. **A `task_complete` readout** trained on trajectories, so the stopping decision is learned rather than
   emergent. Both failure buckets collapse into this one missing signal.
3. **Text-entry-specific supervision.** `type_text` is 1.7% of actions; that alone explains the typed/not-typed
   split above.
4. Online RL — **last**, because MAI-UI's own numbers say it is worth +0.8 per-step at this scale, and it needs
   hundreds of parallel emulators we do not have.

## What this work is worth if none of that happens

Not accuracy. Three things:

1. **The arithmetic.** `P(task) = p^n` and the per-step recovery of published end-to-end scores. No GUI paper
   reports a step-accuracy budget; every one reports end-to-end and lets the reader assume the two are close.
2. **The interface/composition distinction, measured.** 0 invalid actions alongside 81% task failure is a clean
   demonstration that "the action space works" and "the agent works" are different claims.
3. **The cost window (2.11–5.79×).** What an interruption is worth, priced — the only decision-level claim that
   survives every correction made this session.

## If you remember 3 things

1. **18.8% is a one-click agent's score.** The model can tap; it cannot sequence. Nothing else explains the
   pattern as completely.
2. **Typing is the wall.** 14 episodes typed, 1 succeeded, median 25 steps (budget exhausted). 66 did not type,
   14 succeeded, median 12 steps.
3. **The gap is data shape, not hyperparameters.** 28 per-step points of supervision, and MAI-UI's own ablation
   shows RL accounts for 0.8 of them.
