## 1. The bug that made the online number look worse than it was

The 8-task AndroidWorld ablation reported a **25% environment-crash rate**. It was not a crash rate.

Both "crashes" are the two Clock tasks. The evidence: the last recorded step has `error: null` and
`action_ok: true`; the **next episode in the same suite ran ten steps immediately after each one**; stderr
repeats `Could not get a11y tree, retrying`; and the final screenshots show a live ticking stopwatch and a
timer set to 20 h 00 m 85 s. The harness caught an exception from `task.is_successful(env)` and labelled it
`env_crash` without asking whether the device was alive.

Fix: `checker_error` is now recorded separately and `env_crash` requires a failed liveness probe. Two
regression tests; **110 passed, 35 skipped**. Paper §5.7 corrected. The deeper finding is that the harness
was measuring the accessibility dump on an animating screen, not the model.

Full note: `notes/2026-11-07-clock-checker-not-crash.md`.

## 3. The cost window: what the gate is actually worth

An accuracy tie is only interesting if the gate changes the decision. Using the saved artifacts only:

| comparison | E2@938 | E2@705 |
|---|---|---|
| gate beats always-act when `C_err/C_ask` > | 2.11 (2.25 bound) | 2.08 (2.22) |
| gate beats always-ask when < | 5.79 (4.61) | 4.19 (3.47) |

The gate dominates both trivial policies over roughly a **2.1–5.8×** cost window on the fitted checkpoint and
**2.1–4.2×** on the next one. Below ~2×, acting is cheap enough that an interruption is not worth paying for;
above ~6×, asking every time is better.

That is the decision-level form of a tie, and it is now §5.12 of the paper and the anchor of the brief.
Assumption stated everywhere: an ask is answered correctly at a fixed cost. No GUI paper prices the ask.

## 4. Zero-GPU post-processing is closed

A val-fitted `p(status)` gate that switches premature terminations to the best expressible action is
**monotone-harmful at every threshold**; the best rule is "never switch" (val 62.76%, test 63.82%). The 21
premature `status` rows are 5.3 points, but they cannot be thresholded in — only learned.

`benchmarks/results/2026-11-07-status-gate.md`.

## 7. The training run — failed its bar, and explained itself

Pre-registered before the run: retrain from base with E2's exact 938-step recipe, changing only the class mix
(`status` 22.0% → 5.3%). Pass: paired val Δ ≥ +1.5, no gold type losing more than one row.

| Split | Base | Arm | Δ | McNemar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| val (827) | 62.76% | 63.85% | **+1.09** | p = 0.44 |
| test (398) | 64.07% | 60.80% | **−3.27** | p = 0.15 |

**Fails both clauses**, and the two splits disagree in sign. The mechanism is the result: the intervention
removed **27 premature terminations** on val and **26 correct ones** — 51→24 and 120→94. A class weight is one
knob on `p(terminate)`; it cannot separate "not finished" from "finished".

Two things came out of the failure. Grounding improved **without being trained** (+2.67 val, +2.26 test),
because fewer early exits mean more acting steps. And an oracle bound computed beforehand predicted the size
of the effect: a *perfect* progress signal is worth only **+1.26** points on this checkpoint, because the
model's own next-best action rescues just 5 of the 21 premature rows. So the type bucket cannot be won by
re-weighting — the next arm must give the model an explicit `task_complete` readout.

Result note: `benchmarks/results/2026-11-07-status-downweight-arm.md`; paper §5.10.
