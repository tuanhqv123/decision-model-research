# 2026-11-07 — checkpoint ensemble: +14.6 pts of headroom, +1.76 pts achieved

**Question.** Seven distinct checkpoints already exist on disk. Their errors are not identical. How much of the
union can be converted into accuracy, honestly?

## The inventory (verified on disk, not assumed)

| run | test acc | weights on disk? |
|---|---|---|
| `E2mem` / `gndB1406` | 64.82% | **yes** (`E2-cur15k-V`, `ckpt-groundB`) |
| `E2pure` | 64.07% | **yes** (same `E2-cur15k-V`) |
| `cont500` | 64.57% | **yes** (`E2c-cont-cur8kB`) |
| `cur24k900` | 62.81% | **yes** (`R2b-V-cur24k`; only the final ckpt is saved) |
| `E2@705` | 61.31% | **NO** — predictions only |

| `cont250`, `cur24k1500`, `gndB1056` | 63.82 / 62.31 / 64.07 | near-duplicates, excluded from the independence claim |

No two prediction files are byte-identical (checked by hashing the `(episode, step, correct)` triples), so these
are seven genuinely different runs, not the same run evaluated twice.

## The ceiling

| set | at least one correct | Δ vs best single |
|---|---|---|
| 7 distinct runs | **316/398 = 79.40%** | **+14.58 pts** |
| all 10 (incl. near-duplicates) | 322/398 = 80.90% | +16.08 pts |

The near-duplicates add only 1.5 pts, so the headroom is not an artefact of counting the same run twice.

## What is actually achievable

Everything below is scored by **episode-clustered 5-fold CV**: the fold assignment comes from the 350 episode ids,
and no test row is used to choose a hyper-parameter. That is stricter than the earlier analysis, which fitted a
tolerance on the full test set and reported +2.76 and +3.52 before those were withdrawn.

| Method | accuracy | Δ vs E2pure | p (episode-clustered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E2pure baseline | 64.07% | — | — |
| best single (`E2mem`/`gndB1406`) | 64.82% | +0.75 | — |
| majority vote, exact point | 64.07% | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| majority vote, point ±8 px | 64.57% | +0.50 | — |
| **majority vote, point ±32 px (CV-chosen)** | **65.83%** | **+1.76** | **0.092** |
| pooled per-checkpoint arbiter (2786 examples, logistic) | 65.33% | +1.26 | — |

Vote at ±32 px: **gained 10, lost 3**, episode-clustered CI **[0.00, +3.52]**. Real in direction, not
significant at n = 398.

## Two bugs this replaced, both mine

1. **The label scheme.** The first arbiter asked "which single checkpoint is right" — undefined on the 57% of rows
   where two or more are right. Those rows were dropped, leaving 107 examples for a 20-feature model. Fixed: one
   example per (row × checkpoint) with a per-run correctness label, 2786 examples.
2. **The CV folds.** `folds` held positional indices into the sorted episode list instead of episode ids, so the
   fold mask never matched and CV accuracy came out at 0.5%. Fixed with an `assert` on the fold element type.

Both bugs produced numbers that looked like results. The second one made the arbiter look catastrophically
broken; the first made it look like it overfit. Neither was a modelling finding.

## The actual finding: the combiner is not the bottleneck

**The pooled learned arbiter is worse than plain voting** (+1.26 vs +1.76), with 28× more training signal than
before. That is now the third learned combiner in this programme to lose to a trivial one (hand routing lost to
per-class adjustment, per-class adjustment lost on test, the logistic fusion lost to voting). When a flexible
model with 2,786 examples loses to "round the point and count votes", the information is not in the features —
it is in the **disagreement structure**, and the features do not encode which checkpoint to trust on *this* row.

## Why so little of the 14.6 pts is reachable

The models are highly correlated. Measured on the 7 distinct runs, over the 398 rows:

| rows | count | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| every checkpoint right | **179** | nothing to gain |
| every checkpoint wrong | **82** | unreachable by any ensemble |
| **mixed** (some right, some wrong) | **137** | **the entire decision space** |

So 137 rows are winnable and 261 are fixed. The union ceiling (316 = 179 + 137) is exactly 79.40%, which checks
out. Getting +1.76 means a net +7 rows — converting ~10 of 137 while losing 3 (7%). A perfect arbiter would
convert all 137 and reach 79.40%. The gap between 7% and 100% is the arbiter's accuracy on 137 rows, and that is
where any further work has to land.

## What this licenses

1. **Voting at a coarse point tolerance (±32 px) is a small real gain** (+1.76, p = 0.09) that costs nothing at
   inference beyond N forward passes. At 103 ms per pass, a 7-model vote costs ~720 ms — which is *above* the
   100 ms operating point and therefore **not deployable on-device**. It is a metric improvement, not a product.
2. **The union ceiling is worth publishing as a bound**, not as a method: "seven checkpoints of this family
   contain 79.4% of the answers, and no combiner we tried extracts more than 1.8 points of it" is an honest and
   useful sentence, and it explains why the programme should stop ensembling.
3. **Deployment reality check:** any ensemble breaks the single-pass latency claim. That matters for the paper
   more than +1.76 pts does.
